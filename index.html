Findings

  • No company is running a clinical interception trial with a RAS inhibitor. Johns Hopkins investigators reported this in July 2026, and Revolution Medicines lists only metastatic and post-surgical programs. Dr. Than's preclinical data (median survival above one year, against under five months in untreated controls) is the evidence a sponsor would weigh.
  • Penn's interception program is funded through its senior investigators. R01CA303474 to Stanger and Vonderheide is the one Penn project among 42 NIH projects on pancreatic interception or prevention since FY2022. NIH RePORTER lists no award with Dr. Than as principal investigator; Dr. Than joined the faculty in July 2026, so career-development and first-independent-award mechanisms apply.
  • Philanthropic interception funding in pancreatic cancer concentrates on mutant KRAS. Lustgarten committed $22.8 million in FY2026, $8.6 million more than in FY2025, with 14 percent of its 62 active grants in early detection and interception.
  • Industry funding for preventive vaccines in people with inherited risk goes to Lynch syndrome. Nouscom and Moderna fund Lynch programs; no BRCA or pancreatic counterpart was found.
  • No corporate foundation funds cancer prevention research. The foundations of Eli Lilly, Amgen, AstraZeneca, Genentech, Merck and Pfizer were searched; none funds prevention research, and their research routes run through the companies' investigator programs. Pfizer's investigator-sponsored research list for talazoparib names pancreatic, breast and ovarian cancer as out of scope (checked 5 October 2026).

The assumption the result depends on most

"Interested" was defined as an award, trial, open call, or stated priority since 2022. Under that definition, KRAS drug makers without prevention activity (Incyte, Astellas, Lilly, Bristol Myers Squibb) fall outside the ranked lists. A looser definition would move Incyte and Astellas into the industry list.
